How We Organize Work

A guide for everyone at Justuju

The Big Picture

At Justuju, work starts with a purpose and ends with execution.

A group of people decide why something needs to happen.

Then the people with the right skills make it happen.

These are two different things — and our system keeps them separate on purpose.

Branches

Why does this work exist?

Our Branches

A branch is a strategic stream. It represents something Justuju has collectively decided matters.

  • Team Bonding — strengthening relationships and shared experiences
  • Collective Work — running the organization day-to-day
  • Networking, Outreach and Revenue Generation — connecting with the world and sustaining ourselves
  • Perspective Building — deepening our understanding of justice, equity, and leadership
  • Systems and Policy Strengthening — building organizational structures that work
  • Learning and Capacity Building — growing our individual and collective skills

What Branches Do

Branches do not execute work directly. They initiate work.

A branch says: "This matters. Let's make it happen."

Each branch has an anchor — a person (rotated every 3 months) who ensures the branch's work stays visible and on track.

The anchor is not a manager. They are a facilitator and accountability point.

What specifically are we doing?

What is an Initiative?

An initiative is a concrete project or program. It is the bridge between a branch's purpose and a function's execution.

  • CCL App — initiated by Networking, Outreach and Revenue Generation
  • Anti-oppressive perspectives — initiated by Perspective Building
  • Stewardship Council Strengthening — initiated by Systems and Policy Strengthening
  • Problem Solving Capacity (Math and DSA) — initiated by Learning and Capacity Building

An Initiative Spans Functions

An initiative is always owned by a branch but executed by one or more functions.

Example: CCL App

  • Software Engineering builds the application
  • Finance handles billing and payments
  • Administration manages vendor contracts

Each function picks up their part. Tasks live in each function's backlog, planned in each function's sprint, following each function's workflow.

Initiative Ownership

The initiative itself has an owner (or a small committee) who tracks overall progress across all functions.

Branch Anchor

Asks: "Is this initiative meeting its purpose?"

Initiative Owner

Asks: "Is the work across all functions on track?"

The Flow — Step by Step

  • A branch identifies a need
  • An initiative is created to address that need
  • Tasks are created in the relevant function backlogs
  • Each function plans and executes the tasks in their own rhythm
  • The initiative owner tracks progress across all functions
  • The branch anchor reviews whether the initiative is meeting its purpose

What About Internal Work?

Sometimes a function has work that doesn't come from any branch.

Examples:

  • Software Engineering refactoring a codebase
  • Administration setting up a new filing system
  • Building shared infrastructure across projects

This is normal. We call this sustaining work. It lives in the function's backlog like any other task. It is simply not linked to any initiative.

Planning Rhythm

How we stay in sync

Planning Rhythm

The cadence depends on each function's sprint duration — weekly, fortnightly, or monthly.

Sprint Start — Function Planning

Each function reviews their backlog: What needs doing? What's blocked? Who's working on what?

This is where execution is planned.

During the Sprint — Async Updates

Quick status updates in Mattermost: Done, In progress, Blocked.

Monthly or Quarterly — Branch Review

Are our initiatives moving? Are we meeting our goals? What needs collective attention?

This is where purpose is checked against progress.
