Part 3: Getting Money

Two main ways organizations get money

Option 1: Loan

What it is: Borrowing money that you must return

  • Must pay back the money (usually with interest)
  • The lender has no say in how you run things
  • After you pay back, all surplus is yours

Option 2: Investment

What it is: Getting money from someone who becomes a part-owner

  • Don't have to pay back
  • Investor gets a share of profits
  • Investor may have a say in decisions

Simple Example

Shanti wants to start a pakoda stall. She needs ₹50,000.

If she takes a loan

  • Must return ₹50,000 + interest
  • All profit after that is hers
  • She makes all decisions

If she gets investment

  • Doesn't return the ₹50,000
  • Shares profit with investor
  • Investor may help with decisions

What About Surplus/Profit?

With a Loan: Pay back the loan first, then all surplus belongs to the organization

With Investment: Surplus/profit is shared between the organization and investors

Part 5: Making the Choice

What each option means for us

If It's a Loan

  • Justuju must pay back the money to Rook N Roll
  • All surplus after repayment belongs to the team
  • You own and control Justuju
  • Clear path to cooperative ownership

If It's an Investment

  • Rook N Roll owns part (or whole?) of Justuju
  • Surplus/profits are shared with Rook N Roll
  • May have input in major decisions
  • Need to negotiate transition to cooperative
